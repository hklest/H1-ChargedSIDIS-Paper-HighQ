\author{H1 Collaboration}
\date{\draft{DRAFT, \today}}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Cross sections for the semi-inclusive production of charged hadrons in deep-inelastic electron--proton scattering are measured at high negative four-momentum transfer squared, $150<\Qsq<20\,000~\GeVsq$, and inelasticity $0.1<y<0.7$. The data were recorded with the H1 detector at HERA at a centre-of-mass energy of $319~\GeV$ and correspond to an integrated luminosity of $351~\pbinv$. Positively and negatively charged hadrons are measured separately, without particle identification, as functions of $\Qsq$, Bjorken $x$, the hadron energy fraction $z$, and the hadron transverse momentum $\pTh$ with respect to the virtual photon in the photon--proton centre-of-mass frame, in 245 bins per charge. The measurement is corrected for detector effects by iterative Bayesian unfolding and for QED radiation from the lepton line to the Born level of single-photon exchange. Integrated over the measured phase space, the cross sections are $10.96~\nb$ for $\hplus$ and $10.65~\nb$ for $\hminus$, and the median total uncertainty is 8.6\% for $\hplus$ and 9.3\% for $\hminus$. The $\pTh$ spectra harden with increasing $z$, and the ratio of positive to negative hadron production rises with $x$ and generally with $z$. Integrated over $z>0.2$, it grows from 1.02 at the lowest $x$ to 1.49 at $\Qsq>1500~\GeVsq$. The data are compared with Monte Carlo event generators and with a global fit of transverse-momentum-dependent parton distributions and fragmentation functions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

In semi-inclusive deep-inelastic scattering (SIDIS), $ep\to e'hX$, a hadron $h$ is measured in coincidence with the scattered lepton. Its energy fraction $z$ and its transverse momentum $\pTh$ relative to the virtual photon give access to the transverse motion of quarks in the proton and in hadronization. For $\pTh\ll zQ$, transverse-momentum-dependent (TMD) factorization expresses the cross section through TMD parton distribution functions and fragmentation functions, whose dependence on the hard scale $Q$ is predicted by QCD evolution~\cite{Collins:2011zzd,Scimemi:2019cmh}. For $\pTh\sim zQ$, collinear factorization applies instead. SIDIS is the process in which TMD distributions and fragmentation functions enter together, and it is a central part of the physics programme of the future Electron-Ion Collider~\cite{AbdulKhalek:2021gbh}.

Global analyses extract unpolarized TMDs at N$^{3}$LL accuracy and beyond~\cite{Bacchetta:2022awv,Bacchetta:2024qre,Moos:2025exp}. Their SIDIS input consists of the charged-hadron multiplicities from the fixed-target experiments HERMES~\cite{HERMES:2012uyd} and COMPASS~\cite{COMPASS:2017mvk}, which lie at $\Qsq$ below about $10^{2}~\GeVsq$. The constraints at higher scales come from Drell--Yan production, which does not involve fragmentation. At HERA, charged-particle production at high $\Qsq$ has been studied through scaled-momentum spectra in the Breit frame, integrated over transverse momentum~\cite{ZEUS:2002vaz}, and through azimuthal asymmetries~\cite{ZEUS:2000esx}. Charged-particle spectra and multiplicity distributions were measured at $5<\Qsq<100~\GeVsq$~\cite{H1:2013qsm,H1:2020zpd}.

This paper presents cross sections for inclusive charged-hadron production in SIDIS at $150<\Qsq<20\,000~\GeVsq$, measured simultaneously as functions of $\Qsq$, $x$, $z$, and $\pTh$ and separately for positive and negative hadrons. The cross sections are given at the Born level and are directly comparable with calculations. The measurement extends the $\Qsq$ range of SIDIS by more than two orders of magnitude (Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}). The main results are summarized in a companion letter~\draft{[cite letter]}.

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:kinematics} defines the kinematic variables and the measured cross section. The detector, the data and simulated samples, and the selection are described in Sec.~\ref{sec:experiment}. Backgrounds, the unfolding, and the radiative correction are discussed in Secs.~\ref{sec:backgrounds}--\ref{sec:radcorr}, and the systematic uncertainties in Sec.~\ref{sec:systematics}. The results are presented in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} and summarized in Sec.~\ref{sec:summary}. The cross sections are tabulated in the appendix.

\section{Kinematics and observables}
\label{sec:kinematics}

With $k$ and $k'$ the four-momenta of the incoming and scattered lepton, $P$ that of the incoming proton, and $P_{h}$ that of the measured hadron, the DIS variables are
\begin{equation}
  q=k-k', \qquad \Qsq=-q^{2}, \qquad x=\frac{\Qsq}{2P\cdot q}, \qquad y=\frac{P\cdot q}{P\cdot k}, \qquad W^{2}=(P+q)^{2}.
  \label{eq:dis}
\end{equation}
The hadron is characterized by
\begin{equation}
  z=\frac{P\cdot P_{h}}{P\cdot q}, \qquad \pTh\equiv\left|\vec{P}_{h,T}\right|_{\gamma^{*}p},
  \label{eq:sidis}
\end{equation}
where $\pTh$ is the transverse momentum of the hadron with respect to the virtual-photon direction in the $\gamma^{*}p$ centre-of-mass frame. Hadrons are measured in the current hemisphere of this frame, into which the struck quark fragments. This corresponds to $x_{F}=2\,\vec{p}^{\,*}_{h}\cdot\hat{P}^{*}/W<0$, where $\vec{p}^{\,*}_{h}$ is the hadron momentum in the $\gamma^{*}p$ centre-of-mass frame and $\hat{P}^{*}=-\hat{q}^{*}$ the proton direction in that frame.

The measured observable is the Born-level cross section in a bin of $(\Qsq,x,z,\pTh)$,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}=\frac{\Cgam\,N^{\mathrm{unf}}}{\Lumi},
  \qquad
  \frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}\Qsq\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}\pTh}=\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}}{\Delta\Qsq\,\Delta x\,\Delta z\,\Delta\pTh},
  \label{eq:xsec}
\end{equation}
where $N^{\mathrm{unf}}$ is the number of hadrons in the bin after correction for detector effects, $\Lumi$ the integrated luminosity, and $\Cgam$ the radiative correction of Sec.~\ref{sec:radcorr}. The tables give $\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}$ together with the bin volume; the figures show the bin-averaged density. A prediction must be integrated over the same four-dimensional bin before comparison. For the highest-$\Qsq$ cell, the upper $x$ boundary is the physical limit $x=1$.

The particle-level phase space is defined by
\begin{itemize}
  \item $150<\Qsq<20\,000~\GeVsq$ and $0.1<y<0.7$;
  \item charged pions, kaons, protons, and antiprotons, $|\mathrm{PDG}|\in\{211,321,2212\}$, with laboratory pseudorapidity $|\eta|<1.6$, in the current hemisphere of the $\gamma^{*}p$ frame, $x_{F}<0$. Hadrons from decays of short-lived resonances and of heavy-flavour hadrons are included. Decay products of $K^{0}_{S}$, $K^{0}_{L}$, $\Lambda$, $\Sigma^{\pm}$, $\Xi$, and $\Omega$ are excluded. \draft{[Confirm whether the particle-level definition also contains the laboratory $p_{T}>0.15~\GeV$ requirement.]}
\end{itemize}
The Born level is pure single-photon exchange with the fixed fine-structure constant $\alpha(0)=1/137.036$, without $Z$-boson exchange, photon--$Z$ interference, QED radiation, or one-loop corrections, including vacuum polarization. At this level the $e^{+}p$ and $e^{-}p$ cross sections are equal. The measurement uses the luminosity-weighted combination of the $e^{+}p$ and $e^{-}p$ data. \draft{[Quote the $e^{+}p$ and $e^{-}p$ luminosities and the residual lepton-beam polarization of the combined sample.]} Integrated cross sections at the Born level including $Z$-boson exchange are given for reference in Sec.~\ref{sec:radcorr}.

The ratio $R=\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}(\hplus)/\sigma_{\mathrm{bin}}(\hminus)$ is also presented. Because both charges are measured in the same events, $R$ is independent of the inclusive DIS normalization and equals the ratio of the corresponding hadron multiplicities.

\section{Experimental method}
\label{sec:experiment}

\subsection{The H1 detector}

The H1 detector is described in detail in Refs.~\cite{H1:1996jzy,H1:1996cvd}. Charged-particle tracks are reconstructed in the central tracking detector, whose main components are the two central jet chambers (CJC) in a solenoidal magnetic field. The scattered lepton is measured in the liquid-argon (LAr) calorimeter, which also measures the hadronic final state together with the tracking detectors. The hadronic final state is reconstructed with an energy-flow algorithm combining tracks and calorimeter clusters~\cite{Peez:2005Hadroo2}, following the standard H1 high-$\Qsq$ reconstruction and calibration~\cite{Kogler:2011zz,Britzger:2013}. The DIS kinematics are reconstructed with the $e\Sigma$ method~\cite{Bassler:1994uq,Bassler:1997tv}, which combines the scattered-lepton and hadronic-final-state measurements. The variables $\Qsq$, $x$, $y$, $z$, and $\pTh$ are computed from the virtual photon reconstructed in this way, and the particle-level cross sections use the corresponding generator-level variables.

\subsection{Data and simulated samples}

The data were recorded from 2003 to 2007, when HERA collided electrons or positrons of $27.6~\GeV$ with protons of $920~\GeV$, at $\sqrt{s}=319~\GeV$. Four running periods are used, with $e^{+}p$ collisions in 2003--2004 and 2006--2007 and $e^{-}p$ collisions in 2004--2005 and 2006. The combined sample corresponds to an integrated luminosity of $351~\pbinv$, known with an uncertainty of 2.7\%.

Detector effects are modelled with fully simulated samples of neutral-current DIS events generated with DJANGOH~1.4~\cite{Charchula:1994kf} and RAPGAP~3.1~\cite{Jung:1993gf}, both with $\Qsq>60~\GeVsq$ and both using the CTEQ6L parton distributions. In DJANGOH, the hard scattering is generated with LEPTO~\cite{Ingelman:1996mq} and the QCD cascade with the colour-dipole model of ARIADNE~\cite{Lonnblad:1992tz}. RAPGAP combines $\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})$ matrix elements with leading-logarithm parton showers. Both generators include QED radiation from the lepton line through HERACLES~\cite{Kwiatkowski:1990es}. The samples correspond to integrated luminosities of $10.5~\mathrm{fb}^{-1}$ (DJANGOH) and $9.1~\mathrm{fb}^{-1}$ (RAPGAP) and cover the four running periods. They are passed through a GEANT-based~\cite{Brun:1987ma} simulation of the H1 detector and through the same reconstruction and analysis chain as the data. The simulated events are weighted for the trigger and for the hadronic-interaction cross sections in the detector material. RAPGAP provides the nominal detector response and DJANGOH the alternative. A dedicated DJANGOH sample covers $\Qsq<60~\GeVsq$. Photoproduction is simulated with PYTHIA, and elastic $\rho^{0}$ production with DIFFVM.

For the comparison with the data, PYTHIA~8.317~\cite{Bierlich:2022pfr} and SHERPA~3.0.3~\cite{Sherpa:2024mfk} are used at particle level without detector simulation. PYTHIA is run with the dipole shower (\texttt{SpaceShower:\allowbreak dipoleRecoil=on}), Lund string fragmentation, and the NNPDF3.1 LO parton distributions~\cite{Ball:2017nwa}. SHERPA is run with MEPS@NLO merging of MC@NLO matrix elements with up to one extra jet, cluster hadronization, and the NNPDF3.0 NNLO parton distributions~\cite{NNPDF:2014otw}. Both are generated without QED radiation from the lepton line, each with $10^{7}$ events, and analysed with Rivet~\cite{Bierlich:2019rhm} in the particle-level phase space of the measurement. The DJANGOH and RAPGAP predictions are taken from their non-radiative samples.

\subsection{Event selection}

Events are triggered by the scattered lepton in the LAr calorimeter. The data are required to come from periods with all relevant detector components operational and from colliding bunches. The scattered lepton must have an energy $E_{e'}\geq11~\GeV$ and a polar angle $\theta_{e'}<2.7~\mathrm{rad}$. It must lie in well-instrumented regions of the LAr calorimeter. Its impact position along the beam axis satisfies $-190<z_{\mathrm{impact}}<15~\mathrm{cm}$ or $z_{\mathrm{impact}}>25~\mathrm{cm}$, and it must avoid the cracks between calorimeter octants, $2^{\circ}<\phi_{\mathrm{octant}}<43^{\circ}$, unless $z_{\mathrm{impact}}>300~\mathrm{cm}$. A run-dependent veto removes regions with trigger efficiency below 100\%. The event vertex must lie within $35~\mathrm{cm}$ of the nominal interaction point. For leptons with $\theta_{e'}<30^{\circ}$, a vertex reconstructed from central tracks alone is also accepted.

Background from beam--gas interactions, beam-halo muons, and cosmic rays is rejected with the standard H1 timing and topological background finders, combined with the ratio $R_{p_{T}}=p_{T}^{\mathrm{had}}/p_{T}^{e}$ of the transverse momenta of the hadronic final state and the lepton. A single finder flag leads to rejection only if it is corroborated by a significant imbalance, $R_{p_{T}}<0.1$ or $R_{p_{T}}<0.5$ depending on the finder. Events with two simultaneous flags are rejected if $R_{p_{T}}>0.1$. This veto removes 0.29\% of the selected events. QED Compton events are rejected by requiring that the event does not contain two electromagnetic clusters of more than $4~\GeV$ each, with a summed energy above $18~\GeV$, that are back-to-back in azimuth, $-\cos\Delta\phi>0.95$.

The final event selection requires
\begin{equation}
  150<\Qsq<20\,000~\GeVsq, \qquad 0.1<y<0.7, \qquad E-p_{z}>45~\GeV, \qquad |z_{\mathrm{vtx}}|<35~\mathrm{cm},
\end{equation}
where $E-p_{z}$ is the difference of the energy and the longitudinal momentum summed over all final-state particles. It is $2E_{e}=55.2~\GeV$ for fully contained events without initial-state radiation, so the requirement suppresses photoproduction and events with hard initial-state radiation.

\subsection{Track selection}

Charged tracks are selected with laboratory transverse momentum $p_{T}>0.15~\GeV$ and pseudorapidity $|\eta|<1.6$. They must have at least five hits in the CJC, a radial track length of at least $5~\mathrm{cm}$, and a distance of closest approach to the vertex satisfying $|\mathrm{DCA}'|\sin\theta<2~\mathrm{cm}$, and they must be fitted to the primary vertex. Each track must lie in the current hemisphere of the $\gamma^{*}p$ frame, $x_{F}<0$, and its $z$ and $\pTh$ must fall inside the analysis binning. All variables defined in the $\gamma^{*}p$ frame, including $x_{F}$, are computed with the virtual photon reconstructed with the $e\Sigma$ method; at generator level, they are computed with the exchanged virtual photon. At reconstruction level, $x_{F}$ is computed with the pion mass for all tracks, and its sign is measured relative to the proton direction in the $\gamma^{*}p$ frame.

Tracks with an opening angle of less than $0.2~\mathrm{rad}$ to the scattered lepton are rejected, as in previous H1 measurements~\cite{H1:2013qsm,H1:2020zpd}. Tracks close to the lepton arise mainly from conversions of photons radiated by the lepton~\cite{Grebenyuk:2012thesis}. The requirement removes 0.11\% of the true hadrons at generator level, which is corrected for in the unfolding.

Accepted tracks are counted as $\hplus$ or $\hminus$ according to their charge, without particle identification. The charge is misreconstructed for 0.19\% of the tracks.

The measurement is binned in seven $(\Qsq,x)$ cells, seven $z$ intervals, and five $\pTh$ intervals, giving 245 bins per charge (Table~\ref{tab:binning}). At detector level, $\pTh$ is binned twice as finely, with edges $0$, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, and $8~\GeV$, giving 490 detector-level bins per charge. Figure~\ref{fig:phasespace} shows the measured phase space in the $(x,\Qsq)$ plane.

\section{Backgrounds}
\label{sec:backgrounds}

One event-level background consists of DIS events from outside the measured phase space that migrate into it through resolution effects or QED radiation. Migrations from $60<\Qsq<150~\GeVsq$ are part of the simulated detector response. Contributions from $\Qsq<60~\GeVsq$ are estimated with the dedicated DJANGOH sample and are negligible, 0.005\% of the selected tracks. Diffractive events are part of the measured cross section and are not subtracted.

Elastic $\rho^{0}$ production, estimated with DIFFVM, contributes 0.008\% of the selected tracks in total, 2.7\% in the interval $0.7<z<1$, and at most 40\% of the selected tracks in a single bin. It is neither contained in the signal simulation nor subtracted, and no uncertainty is assigned to it (Sec.~\ref{sec:systematics}). A fit to a two-track exclusive control sample constrains the DIFFVM normalization to $s=0.08\pm0.15$ of its generator cross section, consistent with the factor $1/7.9=0.13$ obtained by extrapolating measured elastic $\rho^{0}$ cross sections to this $\Qsq$, so the unscaled fractions quoted above are upper bounds on the exclusive content. \draft{[$\phi$, $\omega$, and proton-dissociative $\rho^{0}$ production are not yet quantified.]}

At track level, three background classes are considered. They are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:bgfrac} as fractions of the selected tracks.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Electrons and positrons from photon conversions and from Dalitz and semileptonic decays. Misidentified tracks from the scattered lepton are largely removed by the opening-angle requirement.
  \item Daughters of decays of long-lived neutral hadrons, $K^{0}_{S}$, $K^{0}_{L}$, and $\Lambda$, identified in the simulation by a $K^{0}$, $\bar{K}^{0}$, $K^{0}_{S}$, $K^{0}_{L}$, $\Lambda$, or $\bar{\Lambda}$ ancestor.
  \item Secondary particles from hadronic interactions in the detector material.
\end{enumerate}
The first two classes are concentrated at low $z$. They and the small low-$\Qsq$ contribution are estimated as the average of the DJANGOH and RAPGAP predictions and are subtracted from the measured distributions before unfolding. Of the $3\,877\,298$ selected tracks, they account for $281\,326$, or 7.3\%. The detector-interaction class is removed within the unfolding through the fraction of unmatched tracks in each detector-level bin (Sec.~\ref{sec:unfolding}), so it is not subtracted separately. Charged kaons that decay in flight, and charged kaons produced in detector interactions whose parent is reconstructed, are kept as signal.

Fits of the electron and neutral-hadron templates to data control regions, at large distance of closest approach, do not constrain the two normalizations independently. A $\pm100\%$ uncertainty, fully correlated across bins and charges, is therefore assigned to the subtracted yield (Sec.~\ref{sec:systematics}).

\section{Unfolding}
\label{sec:unfolding}

Detector effects are corrected with iterative Bayesian unfolding~\cite{DAgostini:1995fv} using four iterations. Positive and negative hadrons are unfolded separately; a track reconstructed with the wrong charge is treated as an unmatched track in one response and as a lost hadron in the other. The response matrix of each charge is built from RAPGAP. It maps the 490 detector-level bins, plus one row for hadrons lost from the detector-level phase space, onto the 245 particle-level bins, plus one column for detector-level tracks without a matching hadron. It therefore accounts for efficiencies, bin-to-bin migrations, and migrations into and out of the measured phase space. The detector-level opening-angle requirement is applied to the reconstructed tracks only, so that matched hadrons removed by it populate the loss row. The prior is the particle-level distribution of RAPGAP, including the hadrons lost from the detector-level phase space. The unfolding is implemented in a dedicated program and agrees with RooUnfold~\cite{Adye:2011gm} to numerical precision, with a maximum relative difference of $2.6\times10^{-15}$ for both the RAPGAP and DJANGOH responses.

The binning was chosen to give adequate purity and acceptance in nearly all bins. The purity, the fraction of tracks reconstructed in a bin that originate from the same particle-level bin, is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:purity} for the RAPGAP response and, for comparison, the DJANGOH response. It is lowest at high $x$ and high $\pTh$, with a minimum of 9.6\% for $\hplus$ and 10.8\% for $\hminus$. In this region the resolution in the virtual-photon direction is worse, so tracks migrate from the much more populated low-$\pTh$ region into the high-$\pTh$ tail. These bins carry large uncertainties. \draft{[The analysis note foresees that they may be removed from the published set.]}

In the cross-generator closure tests of Fig.~\ref{fig:closure}, detector-level DJANGOH events are unfolded with the RAPGAP response and vice versa, and the results are compared with the particle-level predictions of the generator that provided the input. Bin-by-bin correction factors from RAPGAP, applied to DJANGOH, reproduce the integrated cross sections to 0.12\% for $\hplus$ and 0.07\% for $\hminus$. As a cross-check of the nominal result, the data are also unfolded with the regularized method of TUnfold~\cite{Schmitt:2012kp}, with $\tau=10^{-5}$; the systematic variations are not propagated through this cross-check. It agrees with the nominal result, with $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=0.37$ ($\hplus$) and 0.31 ($\hminus$) using the diagonal uncertainties. The 20 bins in which TUnfold returns a negative value are compatible with the nominal result, with a maximum pull of 1.7. In 1000 pseudo-experiments, the median bias is $-0.04\%$ for TUnfold and negligible for the nominal method.

\section{Radiative corrections}
\label{sec:radcorr}

QED radiation from the incoming or outgoing lepton changes the reconstructed kinematics. The unfolded cross sections are corrected to the Born level of Sec.~\ref{sec:kinematics} bin by bin with
\begin{equation}
  \Cgam(\mathrm{bin})=\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{Born}}(\mathrm{bin})}{\sigma_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathrm{bin})}, \qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{Born}}(\mathrm{bin})=\Cgam(\mathrm{bin})\,\sigma_{\mathrm{unfolded}}(\mathrm{bin}),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is the particle-level cross section predicted by DJANGOH with QED radiation from the lepton line and $\sigma_{\mathrm{Born}}$ that of the same generator at the Born level. Both samples are processed with identical selection, binning, hadron definition, and weighting, and each is normalized to its own simulated luminosity. The selection is the particle-level phase space of Sec.~\ref{sec:kinematics}, together with $E-p_{z}>45~\GeV$ computed from the generated final state. This requirement is applied identically to both samples. \draft{[Confirm that the generator-level $E-p_{z}$ is computed from all final-state particles, so that the requirement is inactive at Born level.]} Both beam charges are combined, as in the data.

Weighted with the unfolded data and integrated over the measured phase space, the correction is $\Cgam=0.977$ for both $\hplus$ and $\hminus$, with values between 0.71 and 1.03 in individual bins. No RAPGAP sample without $Z$-boson exchange is available. The uncertainty is therefore taken from the correction including $Z$ exchange, $\Crad=\sigma_{\mathrm{nonrad}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{rad}}$, where $\sigma_{\mathrm{nonrad}}$ is the cross section with $\gamma$ and $Z$ exchange and with QED radiation switched off. The bin-by-bin difference between the DJANGOH and RAPGAP calculations of $\Crad$, with a median of 0.27\% ($\hplus$) and 0.29\% ($\hminus$) and a maximum of 6.2\%, is taken as an uncertainty. The statistical uncertainty of $\Crad$, with a median of 0.41\% ($\hplus$) and 0.44\% ($\hminus$), is included separately. The correction $\Cgam$ carries both components unchanged as relative uncertainties. The correction is valid for the combined $e^{\pm}p$ sample only.

Applying $\Crad$ in place of $\Cgam$ maps the measured cross sections to the Born level of the full neutral-current process, with $\gamma$ and $Z$ exchange, whereas $\Cgam$ maps them to the Born level of single-photon exchange at fixed $\alpha(0)$. Weighted with the unfolded data, $\Crad$ integrates to 1.072 for both charges. The corresponding integrated cross sections, given for reference only, are $12.03~\nb$ ($\hplus$) and $11.68~\nb$ ($\hminus$). The difference between the two conventions arises from the running of $\alpha$, which is absent at fixed $\alpha(0)$, and from $Z$-boson exchange.

\section{Systematic uncertainties}
\label{sec:systematics}

The uncertainty components and their median values are listed in Table~\ref{tab:syst}, and their dependence on $\pTh$ is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:syst}. All relative components refer to the unfolded result of the background-subtracted data.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textit{Statistical uncertainty.} It is the standard deviation of the results from 1000 Poisson replicas of the data, each propagated through the unfolding.
  \item \textit{Unfolding.} The model uncertainty is the difference between results with the RAPGAP and DJANGOH responses. The regularization uncertainty is the change for $\pm1$ iteration. The dependence on the prior distribution of the hadron azimuth $\phi_{h}$ is estimated by unfolding with a prior that is flat in $\phi_{h}$ and taking half of the larger deviation of the two generators. The statistical uncertainty of the simulated response matrix is propagated through the unfolding. A per-bin closure uncertainty, correlated between the charges, is derived from toy studies with two particle-level models, DJANGOH and a data-like truth, as the rms of the mean toy bias relative to the unfolded yield; it is calibrated on the coverage of held-out truth models.
  \item \textit{Scattered lepton and hadronic final state.} The lepton energy is shifted by 1\% and its polar angle by 1~mrad. The hadronic energy scales of jets and of the remaining clusters are each shifted by 2\%, and the hadronic polar angle by 1~mrad. Each shift is applied to the simulation and propagated through the response and the unfolding, and the absolute difference is taken as a symmetric uncertainty. The resolution of the hadronic $E-p_{z}$ is varied by an additional smearing in the simulation. The residual difference between data and simulation in the rate of migrations across the $\Qsq$ and $x$ bin edges is also taken as an uncertainty. With the $e\Sigma$ method the hadronic-final-state shifts enter through the reconstructed kinematics. They also enter through the $E-p_{z}$ requirement, which is re-evaluated in each shifted simulation. Summed over the six shifted samples, each of about $2.6\times10^{7}$ reconstructed events, $60\,039$ events enter and $63\,695$ leave the selection, mainly for the upward and downward shifts, respectively.
  \item \textit{Tracking efficiency.} The track-reconstruction efficiency is shifted by 0.5\%.
  \item \textit{Detector interactions.} The effect of removing the detector-interaction class within the response is taken as the quadrature sum of its model and statistical parts. The simulated probability for hadrons to be lost in the detector material is varied by $\pm100\%$, with lost hadrons treated as unreconstructed, and the normalization of the contribution of non-primary tracks is varied.
  \item \textit{Radiative correction.} The uncertainty combines the DJANGOH--RAPGAP difference and the statistical uncertainty of the correction including $Z$ exchange, carried unchanged by $\Cgam$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:radcorr}).
  \item \textit{Background subtraction.} The subtracted electron and neutral-hadron yields carry a $\pm100\%$ normalization uncertainty, fully correlated across bins and charges. The same nuisance also varies the daughters of weakly decaying hyperons that are not in the subtracted neutral-hadron class and are removed within the unfolding. This term is combined with half the difference between the DJANGOH and RAPGAP templates, the difference between subtraction before and after unfolding, and the model dependence of the unmatched-track fraction. A $\pm30\%$ variation of the low-$\Qsq$ contribution is treated as a separate component, fully correlated across bins and charges.
  \item \textit{Elastic $\rho^{0}$ production.} This contribution, estimated with DIFFVM, is neither contained in the signal simulation nor subtracted. No uncertainty is assigned, since some theoretical frameworks include diffractive $\rho^{0}$ production in the semi-inclusive cross section. For a subtraction $\sigma\to\sigma(1-\epsilon_{i}f_{i})$ of the raw and Born cross sections alike, with $\epsilon_{i}f_{i}$ fully correlated across bins and charges ($\rho_{ij}=\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}$), the estimated elastic-$\rho^{0}$ fraction $f_{i}$ at the data-tuned DIFFVM scale, its recommended uncertainty, the conservative 95\% bound, and the sign $\epsilon_{i}$ are given for each bin with the cross sections (supplementary material).
  \item \textit{Luminosity.} The luminosity uncertainty is 2.7\%, fully correlated across bins and charges.
\end{itemize}

The $\pm100\%$ normalization uncertainty of the background subtraction is motivated by two studies. First, the control-region fits to the tail of the distance-of-closest-approach distribution do not constrain the two normalizations independently (Sec.~\ref{sec:backgrounds}). Second, when a tighter requirement, $|\mathrm{DCA}'|<0.3~\mathrm{cm}$, is propagated consistently through data, response, templates, and unfolding, 86 bins shift by more than three standard deviations, evaluated with 1000 pseudo-experiments, whereas 1.2 are expected. The integrated cross sections change by $-2.4\%$ ($\hplus$) and $-1.4\%$ ($\hminus$). All but two of the significant shifts are covered by the $\pm100\%$ envelope. The two exceptions require 114\% of the envelope for $\hplus$ at $700<\Qsq<1500~\GeVsq$, $0.034<x<0.150$, $z<0.05$, $0.5<\pTh<1~\GeV$, and 104\% for $\hminus$ at $300<\Qsq<700~\GeVsq$, $0.016<x<0.069$, $0.3<z<0.5$, $4<\pTh<8~\GeV$; the $\pm100\%$ uncertainty is retained. Integrated over the measured phase space, the background-normalization uncertainty is 9.1\% ($\hplus$) and 9.4\% ($\hminus$).

The total uncertainty is the quadrature sum of all components. Its median is 8.6\% for $\hplus$ and 9.3\% for $\hminus$, and it ranges from 4.7\% to 108\% ($\hplus$) and 131\% ($\hminus$); 57\% of the bins have a total uncertainty below 10\%. The luminosity, background-normalization, and low-$\Qsq$ components are fully correlated across all bins and between the charges. The correlations of the other components between bins and between the charges are included in the covariance matrices, which are positive definite and are provided with the tabulated results.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Cross sections}

The Born-level cross sections are shown in Figs.~\ref{fig:xsec_hp} and~\ref{fig:xsec_hm} as functions of $\pTh$ in the seven $z$ intervals of each $(\Qsq,x)$ cell, and are listed in Tables~\ref{tab:sidis-crosssections-noZ-pos-S0x0}--\ref{tab:sidis-crosssections-noZ-neg-S3x0} of the appendix. The cross-section densities span eleven orders of magnitude. Integrated over the measured phase space, the cross sections are
\begin{equation}
  \sigma(\hplus)=10.96~\nb, \qquad \sigma(\hminus)=10.65~\nb,
\end{equation}
with normalization uncertainties of 2.7\% from the luminosity and $\pm1.0~\nb$ (9.1\%) for $\hplus$ and $\pm1.0~\nb$ (9.4\%) for $\hminus$ from the background subtraction. About 71\% of the cross section lies at $z<0.05$. Without the radiative correction, the integrated cross sections are $11.22~\nb$ and $10.90~\nb$. The $\pTh$ spectra harden markedly with increasing $z$. Between the lowest and highest $\pTh$ interval, the cross section falls by more than three orders of magnitude at $z<0.05$, but only by about one order of magnitude at $z>0.5$. This is expected when the hadron inherits a growing fraction $z$ of the transverse momentum of the fragmenting quark.

\subsection{Charge ratio}

Table~\ref{tab:ratio} gives the ratio $R=\sigma(\hplus)/\sigma(\hminus)$ integrated over $\pTh$ for $z>0.2$, and Fig.~\ref{fig:ratio} shows it in all bins of the measurement, at the same Born level of single-photon exchange. In the lowest-$x$ cell, $\hplus$ and $\hminus$ are produced at nearly equal rates, $R=1.02\pm0.01\pm0.01$, as expected when sea quarks and antiquarks are struck at similar rates. Within each $\Qsq$ interval, $R$ increases with $x$ and generally with $z$. For $\Qsq>1500~\GeVsq$ it reaches $1.49\pm0.03\pm0.02$ for $z>0.2$ and $2.54\pm0.24\pm0.12$ for $z>0.5$. This pattern reflects the growing dominance of valence $u$ quarks at large $x$ and the preference of a $u$ quark to fragment into a positive leading hadron at large $z$. The uncertainties of $R$ are obtained from the full covariance matrix, including the correlations between the two charges, so that the luminosity uncertainty cancels. The statistical uncertainty includes the correlation between the charges, and the systematic uncertainty includes the statistical uncertainty of the simulation.

\subsection{Comparison with predictions}

The data are compared with the predictions of PYTHIA~8, SHERPA~3, DJANGOH, and RAPGAP, all without QED radiation from the lepton line (Fig.~\ref{fig:datamc}), and with the MAPTMD24 fit of TMD parton distributions and fragmentation functions~\cite{Bacchetta:2024qre} (Figs.~\ref{fig:map_hp} and~\ref{fig:map_hm}). The generators combine different parton showers with string (PYTHIA, DJANGOH, RAPGAP) or cluster (SHERPA) hadronization, and their predictions differ substantially across $z$ and $\pTh$. They are evaluated at the Born level with $\gamma$ and $Z$ exchange, and SHERPA for $e^{+}p$ collisions only. For the comparison in Fig.~\ref{fig:datamc}, the data are therefore corrected with $\Crad$ to the same level (Sec.~\ref{sec:radcorr}).

PYTHIA exceeds the data at $\pTh<1~\GeV$ and $z>0.3$, by 10--35\% for $0.3<z<0.5$ and by up to a factor 2--3 at $z>0.7$. The median data/MC ratio over all 490 bins (not all shown) is 0.999 (PYTHIA), 0.998 (SHERPA), 1.114 (RAPGAP), and 1.054 (DJANGOH). SHERPA agrees with the data to a few per cent for $\Qsq<700~\GeVsq$ and lies about 10\% below them for $\Qsq>1500~\GeVsq$ (not shown).

MAPTMD24 provides predictions in 230 of the 490 bins, with none at $z<0.05$ and sparse $\pTh$ coverage at large $z$, and is shown only in those bins. MAPTMD24 lies below the data, with a median MAPTMD24/data ratio of 0.93 over its 230 bins and $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=2541/230$ for both charges with the full covariance (theory band not included). \draft{[$\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ convention of MAPTMD24 to be confirmed.]}

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

Cross sections for the semi-inclusive production of charged hadrons in deep-inelastic $ep$ scattering have been measured with the H1 detector at $150<\Qsq<20\,000~\GeVsq$ and $0.1<y<0.7$, using $351~\pbinv$ of data at $\sqrt{s}=319~\GeV$. The measurement is fully differential in $\Qsq$, $x$, $z$, and $\pTh$, separate for positive and negative hadrons, and corrected to the Born level of single-photon exchange, in 490 bins with median total uncertainties of 8.6\% ($\hplus$) and 9.3\% ($\hminus$). The $\pTh$ spectra harden strongly with $z$. The ratio of positive to negative hadrons rises with $x$ and $z$, from unity at low $x$ to about 2.5 at large $z$ for $\Qsq>1500~\GeVsq$, as expected from valence $u$-quark dominance and favoured fragmentation. The measurement extends SIDIS by more than two orders of magnitude in $\Qsq$, to the scales of the $Z$-boson Drell--Yan data used in global TMD analyses. It provides a high-$\Qsq$ reference for TMD factorization and evolution and for the modelling of hadronization in event generators, ahead of the Electron-Ion Collider.
